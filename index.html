Abstract

Returns hide which learned component of a market maker fails after a regime shift. We diagnose a frozen Transformer running policy mirror descent (PMD) in context: a learned actor plus a learned critic fed six sampled transitions per state–action pair. On a synthetic, exactly solvable market family under fee, volatility, liquidity and toxicity stress, we swap each component for its exact counterpart and measure value capture (uniform 0, optimum 1), not P&L. Across 5 trained runs × 64 paired markets per family, pre-registered two-sided tests show the full loop holds against exact PMD on nominal markets. The actor stays within the holds margin on all nine families (small excess only under severe volatility and toxicity: +0.0035, +0.0062); the critic’s excess is positive everywhere, and a follow-up untrained six-sample average loses 0.70–1.10 times as much. The loop beats HBEST, the best nominal rule, under severe fee and toxicity stress but loses to it on nominal, volatility and illiquidity markets, and to the order-flow rule HIMB under toxicity. From a random start, in-context adaptation loses to the carried old-regime optimum on 6 of 8 stress families. The critic stage should be improved first.

Keywords: market making, in-context reinforcement learning, policy mirror descent, Transformers, market microstructure, regime shift
